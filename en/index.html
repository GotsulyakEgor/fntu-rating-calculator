How the table tennis rating is calculated

The calculator follows the rules of the rating system of the Table Tennis Federation of Ukraine (FNTU). Below is the full calculation, step by step.

1. Basic quantities

  • R — the player's rating: playing strength expressed as a number;
  • N — rating weight: how stable the rating is and how active the player is in competitions;
  • K — tournament coefficient: the higher the level of the tournament, the bigger the coefficient;
  • P — the sum of contributions of all your wins;
  • S — the sum of contributions of all your losses (taken as an absolute value).

2. Contribution of a single match

The contribution depends on the rating difference ΔR between you and your opponent.

SituationRating differenceContribution
Win over a weaker (or equal) player / loss to a stronger (or equal) oneup to 2 inclusive2

Unexpected result: win over a stronger player / loss to a weaker oneany(ΔR + 5) / 3, rounded

A win over an unrated player gives no contribution. If you have neither a rating nor a single win, losses give no contribution either. The contribution of a win is added to P, the contribution of a loss to S.

3. Rating change and weight

ΔR = K · (P − S) · 10 / min(40; N + P + S)
Rafter = Rbefore + ΔR
Nafter = Nbefore + P + SIn the calculator, the weight you gain in one tournament (P + S) is capped at 20.

The denominator never exceeds 40: this is how weight protects the rating from sharp jumps. The bigger the weight, the more slowly the rating changes. The final rating cannot be negative.

4. A new player and the reference rating

A player without a rating starts from the reference rating and zero weight. The reference rating is the lower of two values: the highest rating among the players you beat and the lowest among those you lost to. If there were no wins, no rating is awarded.

Rreference = min( max rating of players beaten ; min rating of players lost to )

For new players FNTU introduced a correction that stops the rating from rising without real wins:

ΔRnew = K · (P − S − 2) · 10 / min(40; P + S)

5. Worked example

Given: R = 45, N = 12, K = 1. You beat players rated 52, 47 and 44 and lost to a player rated 50.

  • win over 52 — the opponent is stronger by 7: (7 + 5) / 3 = 4;
  • win over 47 — stronger by 2: 2;
  • win over 44 — weaker by 1: 2;
  • loss to 50 — stronger by 5: 1.

P = 4 + 2 + 2 = 8, S = 1. ΔR = 1 · (8 − 1) · 10 / (12 + 8 + 1) = 70 / 21 ≈ +3.3.

New rating: 45 + 3.3 = 48.3. New weight: 12 + 9 = 21.

6. What else affects the rating

  • Weight decreases over time: every month Nnew = N − N² / 300. That is why it is important to play rating tournaments regularly.
  • Men's and women's ratings are related as Rmen = 0.75 · Rwomen. A woman rated 60 corresponds to a man rated 45.
  • A long absence from tournaments lowers rating and weight, and at zero weight the player is removed from the system.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my rating after a table tennis tournament?

Work out the contribution of each match from the rating difference, add up the wins (P) and losses (S), and plug them into ΔR = K·(P−S)·10 / min(40; N+P+S). The calculator above does this automatically.

What is the reference rating?

It is the starting rating of a new player: the lower of two values, the highest rating among the players they beat and the lowest among those they lost to.

What is rating weight?

Weight shows how stable a rating is and how active the player is. After a tournament it grows by P + S and every month it decreases. The bigger the weight, the less the rating changes in a single tournament.

How does FNTU calculate the rating for new players?

A newcomer starts from the reference rating and zero weight, and the rating-change formula has an extra term: ΔR = (P−S−2)·10 / (P+S). This stops the rating from rising without real wins.

Where do I get the tournament coefficient?

The coefficient depends on the level of the tournament. It is set by FNTU and is usually given in the competition regulations. For an ordinary tournament use 1.0.

Can a player's rating be filled in automatically?

Yes. Start typing a surname in the “Find yourself” field or in an opponent's row and the calculator will suggest players from the FNTU rating and fill in their rating and weight. You can type in Latin letters (for example “Tretiak”). The database is updated daily.

Is this an official FNTU service?

No. This is an independent calculator that follows the published rules. See the federation's website for the official rating.
